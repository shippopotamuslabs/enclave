# The Wombo Gospel

Wombo means the opposite. Wombo Superman would be a giant pussy. So WomboAI talks the opposite of how AI talks.

AI copy is smooth, hyped, and scared to death of saying anything specific. Wombo copy is a guy. It says what the button does. Then it shuts up.

This is the voice contract for Shippopotamus, declared canonical by Sean on October 23, 2026. It binds every word a user reads in Stratum, Bloviate, and Enclave, plus the website and the store listings. It sits next to the Palette Gospel and the Button Shape Gospel and carries the same weight they do.

## The enemy catalogue

Sean wrote the field guide already. It lives at `workspace/user/files/Llm-Parody-Persona.md` and it later became Bloviate's AI Psychosis persona (BLO-157), because the fastest way to mock that voice is to hand it a microphone. Read the guide. The short version: em dashes doing the work of four other punctuation marks, "delve" and "tapestry" and "seamless," stakes inflated until a settings screen sounds like a moon landing, and the pivot. God, the pivot. "It's not a to-do app. It's a second brain." That sentence shape dies wherever it breeds.

One tell by itself means little. Dickinson burned through em dashes like they were free. Four tells stacked in one paragraph is a fingerprint, and in 2026 every user alive can read that fingerprint at a glance. They have all spent a year reading machine output.

## Rule one

State the positive case. Dry. In the user's face.

A label says what the thing does. A description says what happens when you tap it and what you get out of it. Sean's example, now law:

"Turns on the chat bubble overlay."

A verb, an object, a period. Copy that defines a thing by what the thing is against is banned in every costume it owns: "without the bloat," "no fluff, just results," "unlike other apps," "finally, a tracker that," "say goodbye to spreadsheets." Each of those spends the user's attention on a strawman. Spend it on the feature.

## The banned list

Cut these on sight.

Words: delve, tapestry, realm, ecosystem and landscape when they mean a field or an industry, robust, seamless, holistic, pivotal, crucial, testament, multifaceted, intricate, vibrant, profound, synergy, paradigm, plethora, myriad, and journey for anything short of an actual trip. Verbs in cosplay: leverage, harness, unlock, empower, elevate, foster, embark, navigate, streamline, underscore, showcase. The human shelf has use, do, make, show, keep, send. Take from that shelf.

Shapes: the negation pivot and its causal cousin, "not because X, but because Y." Staccato triplets. Questions the copy asks and then answers itself for drama ("The result? Devastating."). Hyphenated compound piles: ever-evolving, deeply-interconnected, purpose-driven. Invented labels built by gluing an abstract problem noun to a domain word: the productivity paradox, the orchestration gap, workload creep. Throat-clearing: "It's worth noting," "Importantly," "In today's fast-paced world," plus the magic adverbs quietly, deeply, fundamentally. Teacher voice: "Let's unpack this," "Think of it like a highway," "Imagine a world where." Bold-label bullets in a list a user reads. Endings that announce themselves and repeat the page back to you.

Em dashes get rationed to near zero. A comma does the work. A period does it better. Sean's own documents carry no em dashes. Ours follow his.

## How the voice sounds

Short sentences sit next to long ones, the way a person types when they know what they mean. Specific nouns. Real numbers. "250 for 18" tells a lifter more than "powerful insights" tells anyone. Contractions go everywhere a mouth would put them.

Dry beats loud. Understatement is the house humor, and a line that needs an exclamation point to land has already failed. Emoji stay out of product copy unless Sean typed them himself.

Light is the default. Serious moments get a different gear: an error that can lose a workout, anything touching money, a button that deletes. There the jokes leave and the copy runs plain and exact. What happened. What is still true. What the user does next. Locked in.

Say it like this:

- "Talk. It types."
- "Syncs your workouts to your other devices."
- "That didn't save. Your workout is still on this screen. Try again."
- "Nothing here yet. Log a workout and it shows up."

The other versions live in the field guide. They can stay there.

## Who this binds

Labels, setting descriptions, buttons, empty states, errors, notifications, onboarding, paywall copy, dialog text, the website, the store listings. All of it.

Bloviate's personas get a pass on output and output only. A persona is a character. Aquinas sounds like Aquinas. AI Psychosis sounds like a language model coming apart, on purpose, because that is the joke and the joke is the product working. Bloviate's own screens get no pass. A settings label in Bloviate talks the same way a settings label in Stratum talks.

Internal writing carries no obligation here. Tickets, commits, code comments. The contract binds copy a user reads. This document follows the rules anyway, as a demo.

## The test

Three checks before copy ships. Yes, three. A checklist is allowed.

1. Fingerprint. Run the copy against section 9 of the field guide: dash density, pivot count, banned words, invented labels. Four or more tells together means rewrite.
2. Bar test. Read it out loud. If a person could not say it to a friend without sounding like a press release, rewrite.
3. Swap test. Paste the sentence into a competitor's app. If it fits untouched, it says nothing about ours, so rewrite it with a specific only we can claim.

## Canon mechanics

Same as the visual gospels. This text embeds in full into each repo's contract files, and copy review runs the three checks. There is no theme carve-out for voice. Neo Brutalism keeps its hard shadows. Nobody keeps "seamless."
